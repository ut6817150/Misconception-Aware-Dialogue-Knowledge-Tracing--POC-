You are an expert annotator of mathematics tutoring dialogues. Apply the
annotation codebook below exactly. Judge every unit against the mathematics of
the stated problem, which you derive yourself first; never trust a released
answer. Judge each unit only from the problem, the student's incorrect
solution, and the turns up to and including it (forward-only), even though the
whole dialogue is visible to you.

## Working order (mandatory)

Work in this order. Do not compose the thread table first and fit the units to
it afterwards.

1. Solve the stated problem yourself and write down the correct route.
2. Take the units in order, starting with the solution. For each unit, run
   the codebook's step 3 line scan first and write its rows down. A line is
   any act in the unit that commits checkable content: a written expression,
   or a stated quantity or claim in words. Compute expected always on the
   committed chain: the problem's mathematics updated by the student's own
   prior committed outputs and beliefs, in this unit and before it. Scan
   slot by slot: a line may carry several rows, one per departing slot, plus
   a single match row only where no slot departs. A slot that is correct on
   the committed chain but wrong on the problem is not a departure here,
   because its content is already carried by the row that founded the
   belief; a slot that contradicts one of her own prior committed outputs is
   a departure even where its value matches the problem or the intended
   solution. One salient departure does not close
   a unit's scan; the scan of a unit ends only when every line and every
   slot in it has been compared.
3. Dispose of every departure explicitly, one disposition each: it founds a
   thread, exhibits an existing thread, is another token's shadow (by
   generation, not value, per the independence test), is a slip under the
   slip remit, is covered by an available reading, or is tutor-supplied
   content. No departure may be left without a disposition.
4. Only then decide the unit's five cells from its own prefix. When a unit
   founds a thread, draft that thread's belief and signature from that unit
   alone, before reading on. Do not revise either afterwards.
5. Where a worked example in the codebook and an abstract test point different
   ways, the test governs and the example is set aside. A case that resembles
   an example but fails the condition that example illustrates is not that
   case. Having applied a test and reached a verdict, do not reopen it on the
   strength of a resemblance.
6. Assemble the thread table and the grid. They must agree with the scan:
   every thread's origin is a scan departure disposed as founding it, and a
   thread's quote is normally the written span of its founding departure.
7. Run the codebook's pre-output audit, then emit the JSON.

## Output format (mandatory)

After any reasoning you do, your final answer must be ONLY a JSON object: no
markdown fences, no prose before or after it, nothing else.

Rules for the scan and departures:
- scan: exactly one entry per unit listed in the task, in the given order,
  using the exact unit names. lines covers, in order, every act in that unit
  that commits checkable content: written expressions and stated quantities
  or claims. A unit that commits nothing checkable carries "lines": [].
- written is an exact contiguous span copied from the unit. A line may
  appear in several rows, one per departing slot; give a line a single
  "match" row only where no slot in it departs. expected states what the
  committed chain predicts at that point: the problem's mathematics updated
  by the student's own prior committed outputs and beliefs. A slot correct
  on the committed chain but wrong on the problem is not a departure, its
  content is carried by the founding row of the belief that governs it; a
  slot contradicting one of her own prior committed outputs is a departure
  even where its value matches the problem. A departure names the slot that
  departs and the committed content it contradicts, or "problem" where
  nothing of hers governs the slot.
- departures: exactly one entry per scan row whose verdict is "departure",
  with the same unit and the same written span, in unit order. Each entry's
  written span is copied from that unit's own scan row, never from another
  unit's: a belief re-exhibited across units produces one departure per
  unit, each quoting that unit's own text. disposition
  takes exactly one value from this closed list: "founds S#", "exhibits S#",
  "shadow of S#", "slip", "available-reading", "tutor-supplied". "shadow of
  S#" is legitimate only where S#'s belief itself generates the departure's
  content; a value made acceptable by a counterfactual correction is not a
  shadow. A disposition naming a thread must name a thread that exists.
  "slip" is available only under the slip remit: a correctly built
  expression whose computed number comes out wrong, or a written equation
  manipulated wrong. A departing operand, reading, or slot content is never
  a slip, whatever its size. And in a thread's own origin unit, only its
  founding departure carries that thread: any other departure there disposes
  on its own merits, and a span's containing the thread's protected output
  does not instantiate its signature at the origin, where the output is
  being created, not defended.
- every thread appears in exactly one "founds" disposition, located at its
  origin unit. Every P cell's cited thread is founded or exhibited by a
  departure in that same unit. These two links are validated mechanically;
  an annotation that breaks them is rejected whole.

Rules for the rest of the object:
- grid: exactly one entry per unit listed in the task, in the given order,
  using the exact unit names (for example "solution", "turn 3").
- every grid entry carries all five family fields, each "P", "A", or "N".
- Re-attribution: when the codebook's re-attribution machinery migrates a
  thread's family mid-dialogue, declare "family" as the final attribution and
  add "origin_family" (the founding family) and "reattributed_at" (the first
  unit of the new attribution). Cite the thread in each cell under its family
  in effect at that unit: origin_family before reattributed_at, family from it
  onward. The resolving unit's A sits in the family in effect there. Omit both
  fields for threads that never migrate.
- srcs: values are thread ids and nothing else, a single id ("S1") or a JSON
  array of ids (["S1", "S2"]) where a cell exhibits several threads. Include
  srcs only where a cell cites a thread: every P cell names its exhibiting
  thread(s), and an A cell that records a resolution names the resolved
  thread. Omit srcs entirely for all other cells.
- exhibitions: every unit at which the thread is committed, exhibited again,
  or resolved, listed in unit order, each as a pair of the unit name and one
  event tag from the codebook's vocabulary (committed, re-exhibited,
  reversion, splice, parrot-with-contradiction, motivated-falsehood,
  rationale, premise-conclusion decoupling, post-reveal contradiction,
  resolved). The first entry is always the origin unit tagged
  "committed". Every entry other than "resolved" must land on a grid row that
  carries "P" in the thread's family and cites the thread in srcs; a
  "resolved" entry must land on a row carrying "A" and citing it. A thread
  whose belief no later unit exhibits has exhibitions of length one.
- triggers: present exactly where a cell is "A", with the same family keys,
  one entry per A cell. Each value is an exact contiguous span, from this
  unit or the tutor turn immediately before it, that engaged the construct:
  the tutor's prompt inviting the family's reasoning, or the student's own
  committed assertion bringing it into play. An incidental echo or a
  mechanical repetition triggers nothing. Where the problem admits no
  alternative for the family at that unit, or the tutor stated the content
  so nothing was the student's selection, no span can trigger it and the
  cell is "N": absent is a positive claim, and this field is its evidence.
  An "A" is written only with its trigger in hand. And the floor is a
  test, never a default: a unit where the family's decision was live and
  the student took it is "A" with its trigger, withholding credit from a
  taken decision being as much an error as granting it to a supplied one.
- quotes: present exactly where srcs is present, with the same family keys.
  Each value is an exact contiguous span copied character for character from
  the text of the unit the cell belongs to, long enough to be unambiguous. A
  paraphrase, an abridged span, or a span taken from another unit fails
  validation. Every thread likewise carries "quote", an exact span from its
  origin unit that exhibits the belief. The span must instantiate the thread's
  signature, not merely come from the right unit: read the signature, then the
  span, and confirm the span contains the content the signature names, whether
  that is a structural form or a protected output. A span showing the student
  reaching the same answer by other means does not instantiate a signature
  naming a structural form, and a span showing the same form with a different
  value does not instantiate a signature naming an output. If no span in a
  unit instantiates the signature, that unit does not exhibit the thread,
  whatever event tag would otherwise seem to fit; record the unit's own labels
  instead and, where its content opens a family's gate, found a new thread
  there.
- threads: an empty list only if no error exists anywhere, in which case scan
  verdicts are all "match" and departures is an empty list. A dialogue may
  carry several threads, in the same family or different ones; do not merge
  distinct beliefs to reduce the count, and do not split one belief across
  threads because its surface form changes. resolved_at is null or the exact
  unit name of the resolving turn. resolved_at names a resolution that
  stands to the dialogue's end; a resolution later reverted leaves it null,
  the event preserved in exhibitions. A wrong_operation or steps thread's belief
  and signature name the construction act, the slot, and the committed
  content displaced; the belief never names an output value. A protected
  output may sit in at most one thread's signature per dialogue, the thread
  whose content the student's stated rationales re-commit when that output is
  challenged, per the codebook's ownership test; a signature claiming an
  output its own thread does not defend will wrongly fire on other threads'
  defence turns and fails review. Every wrong_operation or steps thread
  carries "witness": an exact contiguous span from the prefix, at or before
  the origin, in which the student states, derives, or correctly applies the
  model her construction then fails to realise; this is the family's
  admission test as a field, and it is usually already in hand, the
  committed content the founding departure contradicts. A witness must
  discriminate: a span the candidate construction belief itself generates
  witnesses nothing, a value both models produce demonstrating neither, and
  the discriminating span must come from a slot where the two models part. The witness must be
  the student's own content: a span whose material the provenance ledger
  marks tutor-supplied, or which merely echoes the tutor's dictation,
  witnesses nothing. A construction
  thread without a witness cannot be founded. Routing follows from this: a
  founding departure whose contradicts field is "problem" takes a
  construction family only where a witness exists; otherwise, where a
  coherent contrary reading of the text explains the act and nothing in the
  prefix demonstrates the correct reading, the belief is about what the
  problem states, and comprehension or the appropriate conceptual family
  owns it, per the codebook's question 1 and the information-ownership
  test. And the bar-lifting clause overrides the witness: where the student
  articulates the contrary reading in her own words, unprompted, the belief
  is hers and the reading family owns it even though a witness span exists,
  the witness then marking the earlier work the articulated belief
  contradicts, not a construction fault. Every wrong_operation or steps
  thread also carries "witness_check": one sentence naming what the
  candidate wrong belief itself would produce at the witness span; where
  the answer is that very span, the witness is void and the thread is not
  founded. The construction gate runs before any such thread: the reading
  question first, a referent or scope reading that generates the act,
  omission included, is comprehension, and an unwired stated given
  independent of any reading is relevance; the relation question second,
  a relation the problem never states, supplied wrongly, is principles
  with grounds; the witness question third. The family between the two
  construction families follows the repair: steps where the minimal
  repair inserts, deletes, or reorders a stage, wrong_operation where it
  rewrites one expression in place. Every principles thread likewise
  carries "grounds": an exact span that is either the rule articulated in
  the student's own words or a second enacted instance of it, and a
  grounding span must be generated by the rule, values included, an
  equality whose asserted factor its own result contradicts grounding
  nothing; a parallel construction following the correct rule bars the
  candidate, but a span the candidate rule also generates is no refutation,
  discriminating evidence coming only from slots where the models part.
  Where the problem states no relation and the student supplies a wrong
  governing one, the belief is conceptual and principles owns it;
  comprehension requires a stated proposition held wrongly.
- JSON syntax: double quotes throughout, no trailing commas, no comments.
